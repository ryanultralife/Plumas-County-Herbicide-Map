Dear Sierra Valley Groundwater Management District,

I work with the Plumas Grassroots Collective on spraymapca.org, a public, noncommercial map of pesticide use and water data for Plumas County and the Northern Sierra. The County of Sierra recently answered a records request of ours by pointing us to the District as the agency that controls the monitoring wells in the Sierra Valley basin.

Under the California Public Records Act (Gov. Code section 7920.000 et seq.), please provide copies of the following for January 1, 2018 to the present:
1. The list of monitoring wells (and any surface-water sites) the District samples or oversees, with locations (latitude/longitude or a map) and well identifiers.
2. The list of analytes tested at each site, and the sampling frequency.
3. The water-quality results themselves (laboratory reports or a data export), including any pesticide or herbicide analyses.
4. Any groundwater-quality assessments, GSP monitoring reports, or annual reports that summarize these results.

A forward-looking question: none of the monitoring we have found in these watersheds tests for the forestry and rangeland herbicides applied in the surrounding uplands (hexazinone, imazapyr, glyphosate, aminopyralid and others), several of which are water-mobile. If the District has ever screened for these, or would consider adding them, we would be glad to discuss it.

Electronic, machine-readable formats (CSV or Excel) are ideal. This request serves the public interest in transparency and is noncommercial; please waive fees if possible, and contact me first if costs would exceed $25. Under Gov. Code section 7922.535, please let me know within 10 days whether responsive records exist.
