No apology needed, and thank you for laying all of this out. Knowing it is Marin, and that your concern is open space and parkland rather than farmland, makes the picture much clearer.

A few starting points, from what we have learned doing this in Plumas County:

1. A records request can be a short email. Under the California Public Records Act (Gov. Code section 7920.000 et seq.), you can write to each local or state agency and ask for its pesticide and herbicide application records for a date range: date, location, product, active ingredient, amount, and area treated, plus any integrated pest management plan or annual pesticide-use report. The agency has 10 days to tell you whether it has responsive records (Gov. Code section 7922.535). You do not have to know in advance where spraying happened. Asking for every application across all of its lands, by date, avoids having to search area by area.

2. Marin County Parks / Open Space, any municipal water district, and California State Parks are all covered by the Public Records Act. Many agencies also have a public-records portal or a named records contact on their website.

3. Golden Gate National Recreation Area, Muir Woods and Point Reyes are National Park Service units, so those requests go under the federal Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) rather than the state Act. Federal agencies have their own FOIA offices and timelines.

4. California's statewide Pesticide Use Report data comes from county agricultural commissioners. As we said before, the newest year on our map is 2023, and much non-agricultural use is reported only as monthly totals, without locations. That is why going straight to the land-managing agencies is often the better route for open space.

If it would help, we are glad to share the wording we have used in our own requests so you can adapt it rather than start from scratch.

Thank you for your kind words, and for doing this work in Marin.
