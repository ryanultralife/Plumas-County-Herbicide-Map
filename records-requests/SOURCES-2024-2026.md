# Where 2024-2026 pesticide-use data can be had today

Survey run 2026-09-23 (six county sweeps plus one federal sweep; every claimed source was re-checked by an
adversarial verifier: 15 checked, 15 confirmed, 0 refuted). County commissioners run CalAgPermits, which
exports application-level Pesticide Use Report data current to the month; a few publish it, most must be asked.
CDPR publishes the statewide year about 20 months late (2023 arrived Sep 2025; 2024 not yet posted at survey time).

## Self-serve now: application-level use reports covering 2024-2026

### California DPR / County Agricultural Commissioners - whether Forest Service (and other federal) applications appear in county PUR / CalAgPermits exports

- **Publishes use data:** partial (verified-by-download)
- **URL:** https://www.cdpr.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/volume_1_chapter_4.pdf
- **What:** The rule: 3 CCR 6624 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/3-CCR-6624) requires use records from anyone making an 'agricultural use' application (FAC 11408, which covers everything except home, structural, industrial/institutional and vet uses - so forestry, rangeland and rights-of-way are agricultural use), anyone using a 3 CCR 6400 restricted material, and every pest control business; 3 CCR 6627 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/3-CCR-6627) requires those persons to file a Monthly Summary Pesticide Use Report with the CAC of the county where the work was done by the 10th of the following month (fields: applicator, county, month, site/commodity, product with EPA/CA reg. no. and quantity, number of applications, acres/units); CACs key these into CalAgPermits, which feeds DPR. DPR's own enforcement standard, PUE Program Standards Compendium Vol. 1 ch. 4 (rev. 02/2026, https://www.cdpr.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/volume_1_chapter_4.pdf), Section 4.3 'Not reportable pesticide use' expressly lists 'Pesticides applied by federal employees on federal land' alongside home use and FIFRA 25(b) products; the 'Federal facilities' scenario says persons 'hired by a federal agency to perform pest control on federal property' or who lease federal land 'are subject to California's pesticide laws and regulations and must submit PURs to the CAC'. Vol. 1 ch. 1 Section 1.5 (https://www.cdpr.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/volume_1_chapter_1.pdf, pp. 1-24 to 1-28) gives the reasoning: Congress has not required federal agencies to follow state pesticide law and the State cannot penalize them; Executive Order 12088 plus Sierra Club v. Peterson (consolidated with Coalition for Alternatives to Pesticides in Northern California v. Block) make the restricted-materials permit program, registration, storage/disposal, general standards of care (3 CCR 6600-6616), groundwater and TAC programs binding 'pollution control standards' on the Forest Service - and the Forest Service was required to obtain a California restricted-materials permit before using 2,4-D - but DPR does NOT list the use-reporting program (3 CCR 6624-6627) among those standards. Net effect for the map: (1) herbicide applied on National Forest land by a licensed pest control business under FS contract (most release/site-prep and much invasives work) IS in the county PUR/CalAgPermits data, under the contractor's OIN/grower_id, site codes for forest/timberland, rights-of-way or uncultivated non-ag; (2) applications made by Forest Service crews with general-use products are legally non-reportable and will be missing unless the forest files voluntarily (R5 Supp. 2150.3 commits R5 to follow California pesticide law where not in conflict, so some forests may file - not verified); (3) any restricted-material application on NFS land requires a CAC permit, so the CAC has at least a permit and NOI record for it. Press analyses of DPR data (Mother Jones/PBS 2026; Breakthrough Journal, https://www.breakthroughjournal.org/p/the-glyphosate-panic-comes-for-americas) attribute roughly 18% of 2023 forest glyphosate (about 47,000 of 266,000 lb) to National Forest land by spatial overlay of PUR sections on ownership, which is the same method the map can apply to its statewide PUR rows (PLSS section centroid x USFS ownership) to isolate the contractor-reported federal share.
- **How to get current data:** No separate ask is needed for the contractor share: it arrives with each county's CalAgPermits 'Pesticide Use Report' export already being requested; flag rows whose PLSS section falls on NFS/BLM/NPS ownership and whose site is forest/timberland/ROW/uncultivated. For the crew-applied share, ask each forest (via the R5 coordinator or FOIA above) whether it files Monthly Summary PURs with the CAC; where it does, ask the CAC for that operator's OIN so the rows can be tagged 'USFS'. Cite 3 CCR 6624/6627, FAC 11408, and DPR Compendium Vol. 1 ch. 1 sec. 1.5 and ch. 4 sec. 4.3 when a CAC questions why federal-land rows are being requested.
