# Plumas Working Forests — Full Program

**Version:** 2026-08-02 · **Org:** Plumas Grassroots Collective · **Site:** [spraymapca.org](https://www.spraymapca.org/)

---

## 1. Purpose

Describe a **commercially serious** alternative to the dominant post-harvest pathway in the Plumas / Northern Sierra landscape:

> **Clearcut (or heavy regeneration harvest) → broadcast herbicide site prep / release → plant even-aged conifer → spray again until free-to-grow.**

That pathway shows up clearly in California Pesticide Use Reporting on private timberland (glyphosate, hexazinone, imazapyr — see spraymapca Map and Herbicides & Science). Federal land often follows related plantation and fuels-treatment logics; chemical totals on USFS land are incomplete pending FOIA.

This program asks: **Is there an optimized logging system that thins, keeps forest look and health, avoids broadcast spray, and still supports mills and current harvest levels?**

Answer in one line:

> **Possibly yes at landscape and decade scale — but only if multi-entry volume, acres treated, product mix, and cost structure are verified with local data. If they are not, the case fails and we report that.**

---

## 2. Whose decision

| Who | Role |
|---|---|
| **Private timberland owners** (industrial and family) | Own the trees and the land. Choose silviculture, THPs, contractors, markets. |
| **Loggers & mills** | Execute and buy; constrain what is operationally and commercially real. |
| **USFS / other public** | Set prescriptions on public land via NEPA, contracts, and timber sales. |
| **Plumas Grassroots Collective / spraymapca** | Publish chemical transparency + this **case and measurement framework**. We do not run industry operations. |

**Implementation is voluntary on private land.** Public-land policy is a separate track (see POLICY_ASKS.md). Neither track pretends community preference overrides fee-simple ownership or mill contracts.

---

## 3. Problem statement (local)

1. **Herbicide use is large and, in Plumas, rising** after fire-driven reforestation (spraymapca Data & Trends; county PUR detail).
2. Dominant actives include **glyphosate** (volume), **hexazinone** (soil-mobile, groundwater-relevant), **imazapyr** (persistent / non-target plants).
3. The silvicultural purpose of those sprays is usually **competition control for planted conifers** after heavy canopy removal — not an incidental landscaping choice.
4. Community concern (wells, wildlife, forest character) is rational given that pathway. Industry concern (jobs, mill feed, cost of capital) is also rational. A useful program must speak both languages with **the same numbers**.

## 4. The alternative system (summary)

**Name:** Plumas Working Forest Standard (full ops detail in PRESCRIPTION_STANDARD.md).

| Element | Practice |
|---|---|
| **Matrix** | Commercial **variable-density thinning** — remove density and some value trees; leave continuous cover and structure |
| **Gaps** | Small **group openings** (prefer 0.25–1 ac, cap ~2 ac), irregular, not grid clearcuts |
| **Skips** | Untreated patches for structure / wildlife (classic VDT) |
| **Regen** | Prefer **natural regeneration** from retained seed sources; plant where fire or failure requires it |
| **Release** | Hierarchy: design → mechanical/manual → fire where appropriate → **spot** chemical only with disclosure. **Broadcast aerial and soil-residual free-to-grow programs are outside the Standard** |
| **Re-entry** | Plan commercial return **~15–30 years** (site-dependent), not one liquidation per rotation |
| **Wood** | Sawlogs + small wood / biomass / chips as co-products so thinning pays |

This is standard ecological / continuous-cover / multi-aged forestry applied to Sierra mixed-conifer conditions — not a new invention. What is new here is packaging it as a **volume-tested industrial case for Plumas**, tied to the spray data the community already has.

## 5. Volume thesis (and how it can fail)

### Thesis

Landscape board-foot (and biomass) supply can match a clearcut-plantation system **if** one or both hold:

1. **Same acres, more entries:** harvest roughly V/k per entry every R/k years instead of V once every R years; or  
2. **More acres:** California’s fuels/restoration backlog means far more acres *need* thinning than are currently paid for; commercial recovery on those acres can **add** volume without converting residual green forest to sprayed plantations.

### How the thesis fails (we will say so)

The case **does not** support “current harvest levels” if local data show:

- Residual stands after partial cut **cannot** yield mill-grade sorts at needed scale; or  
- Cost per MBF **permanently** exceeds stumpage/mill willingness without subsidies that do not exist; or  
- Re-entry intervals **stretch** so far that decade-scale MBF collapses; or  
- Biomass/small-log markets **disappear** and no substitute offtake exists; or  
- Owners’ capital models require clearcut cash pulses that multi-entry NPV cannot approach **even with** fire/carbon co-payments.

**Rule:** Any public claim of volume parity must cite a 10-year landscape MBF worksheet (method in ECONOMICS.md). Until Plumas mill and CalTREES/FACTS numbers fill that worksheet, figures are **illustrative only**.

Run `python program/tools/volume_sketch.py` for a transparent sketch; replace defaults with local inputs.

## 6. Industry requirements (non-negotiable for a real case)

| Actor | Must get |
|---|---|
| **Loggers** | Enough billable acres/days; equipment and safety fit for partial cuts |
| **Mills** | Predictable annual MBF and usable diameter/sort mix |
| **Biomass / chip** | Reliable small wood if sawlog-only economics fail on thin material |
| **Private owners** | NPV and cash-flow they can live with; regulatory path that is not hostile by design |
| **Public land managers** | Timber and fuels targets that supervisors can defend |

If a pilot or model leaves any of these unsatisfied, **the program reports failure for that context** — not a moral victory.

## 7. Public vs private tracks

### Private land

- Offer: Standard + economics + pilot measurement design.
- Owner decides THP silviculture and whether to use chemical release.
- spraymapca continues to **map** chemical use; it does not seize management.

### Public land (Plumas NF and similar)

- Offer: NEPA alternatives and contract language consistent with the Standard (POLICY_ASKS.md).
- Decision rests with USFS and applicable law.

### Cross-boundary

Same watershed often has both. Alignment is efficient for fire and water; it still requires separate decision-makers.

---

## 8. Relationship to fire and planting

- High-severity burn holes may need **planting**. The Standard allows planting.
- What it rejects as *default industrial practice* is **broadcast chemical free-to-grow as the normal path to a plantation monoculture on sites that still have forest structure or viable natural regen**.
- Fuels reduction that removes small trees and sells wood is **aligned** with this program when it leaves a thinned forest, not a sprayed clearcut.

## 10. What we will not claim

- That multi-entry forestry is always cheaper per MBF than clearcut systems.  
- That every Plumas acre is suitable for group selection.  
- That community preference binds private owners.  
- That federal chemical totals are fully known (they are not).  
- Tax-deductible 501(c)(3) status unless/until an IRS determination exists.
