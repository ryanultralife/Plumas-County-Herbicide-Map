# Plumas Working Forest Standard — Prescription Outline

**Status:** Voluntary technical standard for owners, foresters, and agencies who choose to trial it.  
**Not** a regulation. **Not** a THP template that replaces RPF judgment or Forest Practice Rules.

Site-specific targets (BA, SDI, species) must be set by a qualified forester for Sierra mixed conifer / eastside pine / true fir types as applicable. Ranges below are **starting bands**, not universal cutoffs.

---

## 1. Objectives (all five count)

1. Deliver commercial wood (saw + small wood) on a **repeatable** entry schedule.  
2. Maintain **continuous forest character** after harvest (majority of unit still forested canopy).  
3. Improve or maintain **fire resilience** (reduce ladder fuels and extreme density).  
4. Meet stocking / regen goals **without broadcast herbicide** as a planned tool.  
5. Protect water and non-target vegetation by avoiding soil-mobile and aerial broadcast free-to-grow programs.

If an owner prioritizes (1) alone via clearcut+spray, that is their business decision. This Standard is for those who want (1)–(5) together.

---

## 2. Stand eligibility (guidance)

**Good candidates**

- Overcrowded second-growth mixed conifer with commercial product.  
- Fuels units where commercial thin is already contemplated.  
- Stands with seed source for desired species.  
- Partial high-severity burn with residual green structure.

**Poor candidates / need different design**

- True high-severity moonscape with no seed — may need plant; still can avoid broadcast spray.  
- Stands where only non-commercial material exists and no biomass offtake — economics must be checked.  
- Legal or habitat constraints that prohibit commercial entry (follow law first).

---

## 3. Marking and retention

- Prefer **leave-tree** mark or designation-by-description that field crews can execute consistently.  
- Retain: large/legacy trees of fire-resistant species where present; a snag and down-wood component; oak and other hardwoods where they are part of the stand goal; riparian and unstable areas per rules **plus** any owner-chosen buffers.  
- Document wildlife trees and skip patches on the map.

---

## 4. Matrix thinning

- Thin to a **healthy density band** for the site (use density management diagrams / local USFS or UCANR guidance for the series — do not invent a single county-wide BA).  
- Prefer **from below** for fuels and suppressed removal; add codominant removal where spacing and value require it (VDT “matrix”).  
- Avoid diameter-limit high-grading that leaves only poor residuals.

**Pass check:** After harvest, the matrix reads as **forest**, not clearcut with leave trees.

---

## 5. Gaps (group openings)

| Parameter | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Individual gap size | Prefer **0.25–1.0 ac**; hard soft-cap **~2.0 ac** unless a documented exception (e.g. existing opening, safety) |
| Shape | Irregular; follow microtopography; no rectangular plantation blocks |
| Aggregate gap area in one entry | Keep unit **forest-dominated** — starting target: **gaps &lt; ~20–30%** of unit area in a single entry (forester may justify otherwise; document) |
| Placement | Away from unnecessary stream proximity; use for regen and early seral, not road-side cosmetics only |

Gaps replace large clearcut units as the early-seral tool.

---

## 6. Skips

- Leave **untreated patches** (VDT skips) for structure, refugia, and visual complexity.  
- Map them; do not “clean up” skips after the fact unless hazard requires it.

---

## 7. Regeneration

1. Prefer **natural regeneration** from retained overstory.  
2. Plant where surveys show failure or where burn severity left no seed — **planting is allowed**.  
3. Stocking surveys on a declared schedule (e.g. years 1, 3, 5).  
4. **Do not** write the prescription to require chemical free-to-grow certification as the success definition.

---

## 8. Competition control hierarchy (core chemical rule)

Use the **lowest** step that meets stocking and fire objectives:

| Step | Method |
|---|---|
| 0 | Silvicultural design (shade, residual density, species mix) that reduces brush response |
| 1 | Manual / chainsaw release; hand pile |
| 2 | Mechanical (mastication, brush rake, grapple pile) where soils and residual trees allow |
| 3 | Pile burn / underburn / prescribed fire where legal, safe, and planned |
| 4 | **Spot** herbicide (individual stem, cut-stump, backpack on targets) with product, rate, acres, and map **disclosed** |
| Out | **Broadcast** aerial application; **soil-residual free-to-grow programs** (e.g. hexazinone-class grid) as planned regime; tank-mix campaigns whose purpose is plantation release across whole units |

**Standard compliance:** Steps 0–3 only, or step 4 with public-quality disclosure.  
**Non-compliance:** Planned broadcast free-to-grow chemistry.

Owners may still legally spray under California rules; they simply are **not** operating under this Standard if they broadcast.

---

## 9. Re-entry and wood flow

- Plan the next commercial entry when growth and density justify it (**often 15–30 years**, site-dependent).  
- Record projected MBF for this entry and sketch for the next — feeds ECONOMICS volume parity.  
- Coordinate sorts with the mill (chip, small saw, large saw) **before** the sale if possible.

---

## 10. Operations and infrastructure

- Choose systems suited to partial cuts (CTL, thinning-configured ground, skyline where needed).  
- Minimize new permanent road relative to what a clearcut pulse would build; maintain what multi-entry needs.  
- Slash treatment must meet fire and reforestation goals without defaulting to “spray the unit.”

---

## 11. Documentation package (minimum for a trial unit)

1. Pre-harvest inventory summary (BA, TPA, species, volume estimate).  
2. Mark / description rules and map (matrix, gaps, skips).  
3. Expected MBF and product sorts.  
4. Regen plan (natural / plant).  
5. Release plan with hierarchy steps; chemical = none or spot-only detail.  
6. Post-harvest residual metrics.  
7. Cost and revenue actuals (can be confidential to owner; share aggregates if willing).  
8. Year 3–5 stocking results.

Without (7) and (8), the **industry case cannot be validated**. Confidential business data can stay private; the program only needs enough shared fact to pass or fail the public claim.

---

## 12. Progressive layout (optional “PWF” add-on)

To avoid **scattered multi-entry ops** (loggers jumping the ownership), units may claim **Progressive Working Forest** layout when they also meet:

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| **P1** | Annual commercial acres form a **contiguous front** (connected road-shed / shared boundaries) — not random leapfrog units |
| **P2** | **Designated** skid or skyline corridors mapped and reused on re-entry |
| **P3** | Written **strip or compartment progression** for ≥10 years |
| **P4** | Silviculture still meets §§3–8 (matrix/gaps/skips; no broadcast free-to-grow) |

Full rationale, satellite limits, and boom-corridor / strip systems: [PROGRESSIVE_LAYOUT.md](./PROGRESSIVE_LAYOUT.md).

---

## 13. Conformance statement (optional language)

> “Unit X was harvested under the Plumas Working Forest Standard (version DATE): matrix thin + gaps ≤ 2 ac, skips retained, no broadcast herbicide, regen plan attached.”

Optional: “Progressive layout (P1–P3) applied.”

False claims undermine the program; do not use the name without meeting §8.
