# County of Sierra — water-quality / monitoring PRA response (August 2026)

Public-Records-Act response from the **County of Sierra**, delivered through its
**County Counsel, Prentice Long PC** (Redding — attorney Andrew Pentecost, legal
assistant Kellie Haigh). Received at spraymapca@gmail.com on **2026-08-06**;
responds to our request logged by the county as *"PRA Request Received July 13,
2026 — County Water Quality & Monitoring Data."* The raw response PDF is
**git-ignored** (`Sierra-County-PRA-response-2026-08-06.pdf`).

## What we asked
1. All water-quality monitoring results, domestic-well testing summaries, small
   water-system reports, and raw laboratory testing data for watersheds, public
   water systems, monitoring wells, and testing sites under county jurisdiction
   in Sierra County, Jan 2020–present.
2. Documentation on local environmental hazards, groundwater-quality
   assessments, or watershed-conservation data managed by the county.

## What the county said
- **Request #1 — no county monitoring data.** The county states it *"does not
  generally have regulatory jurisdiction over watersheds,"* including monitoring
  and testing of wells and public water systems. It pointed instead to:
  - the State Water Board **Drinking Water Program** and **GeoTracker**;
  - the **Sierra Valley Groundwater Management District** (which controls the
    monitoring wells the county permitted); and
  - a **Dropbox** link to responsive **State Small Water System** records
    (link in the PDF; **expires 2026-09-05** — pull before then if the raw SSWS
    files are wanted).
  - Note: the Cemetery Water System in Sierra City is inactive → no records.
- **Request #2 — objected.** Refused as vague/overbroad (citing *Rogers v.
  Superior Court*), inviting a narrowed supplemental request. No records produced.
- cc: Elizabeth Morgan, MPH, REHS — Environmental Health Specialist III.

## The finding (surfaced on the site)
A **second, independent jurisdiction** — Sierra County, adjacent to Plumas and
sharing the same forest and watersheds — confirms the project's central gap.
The county does not monitor its watersheds for anything, let alone herbicides;
it defers to the State and the groundwater district. The only water records it
holds are **State Small Water System** drinking-compliance files, which test the
*required* contaminants (bacteria, nitrate), **not** hexazinone, imazapyr,
glyphosate, aminopyralid or the other compounds sprayed in these hills.

Surfaced in the Science tab card **"Is the water tested for these herbicides?
Not yet."** under the sub-head *"A second county, the same answer."*
